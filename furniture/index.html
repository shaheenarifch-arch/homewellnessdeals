How to Choose the Right Sofa or Sectional

A sofa is usually the largest and most-used piece in a home, so it is worth choosing with a plan rather than on looks alone. Start with three questions: who sits here every day, what else happens in this room, and how long do you expect to keep it? A couple who stream shows at night needs something different from a family that naps, hosts game nights and moves every few years.

For everyday comfort, look at seat depth and seat height together. Standard sofas have a seat depth of roughly 20–24 inches; deeper seats of 25 inches and more feel lounge-like and suit people who curl up, while shallower seats support upright sitting and are easier to stand up from. A seat height around 17–19 inches works for most adults.

  • Sofas suit smaller rooms and flexible layouts. They are easy to move and pair with accent chairs.
  • L-shaped sectionals anchor a corner and seat more people in the same footprint as a sofa plus chair.
  • U-shaped sectionals create a conversation pit feel in larger rooms and double as a lounge for movie nights.
  • Sofa beds trade some lounge comfort for a real sleeping surface, which is ideal for apartments and dens.

How to Measure Before You Buy

Most furniture regret comes from measurements, not style. Measure the room, then measure the path the furniture must travel.

  1. Map the footprint. Use painter's tape on the floor to outline the sofa's width and depth. Live with it for a day.
  2. Protect walkways. Leave about 30–36 inches for main walkways and 14–18 inches between the sofa and a coffee table.
  3. Check the route. Measure door widths, hallway corners, stair turns and elevator depth. Compare these against the box or the largest section, not the assembled sofa.
  4. Confirm the orientation. For L-shaped sectionals, check whether the chaise or long side is on the left or right as you face it, or whether it is reversible.
Tip: Modular sectionals arrive in separate pieces, which makes tight stairwells and apartment elevators far easier than a one-piece sofa.

Modular Sectionals vs Traditional Sofas

Modular furniture is made of individual seats, corners and ottomans that connect together. That design brings practical advantages:

  • Reconfigure anytime as an L-shape, U-shape, straight sofa or separate chairs.
  • Easier delivery through narrow doors and stairways.
  • Grow or shrink the layout when you move or rearrange.

Traditional sofas usually feel more tailored and have fewer seams. If you rent, move often or like changing your layout with the seasons, modular pieces tend to be the more flexible long-term choice.

Choosing a Sofa Bed for Guests

A sofa bed turns a living room, den or home office into a guest room. Look for a sleeping surface that suits adults, a mechanism that opens without moving the whole room, and cushions that still feel comfortable as a sofa. Oversized designs give more room for two sleepers. Keep a set of sheets and a cozy blanket nearby so hosting takes minutes.

Common Sofa Buying Mistakes

  • Buying for the showroom, not the room. Large stores make big sofas look small. Always tape out the footprint at home.
  • Ignoring seat depth. Tall people usually prefer deeper seats; shorter people often find very deep seats need extra back pillows.
  • Forgetting the delivery path. Measure door frames, stair turns and elevator depth before you order.
  • Choosing fabric by color alone. Consider pets, kids, sunlight and how often you entertain.
  • Skipping the return policy. Large-item returns can involve pickup fees, so read the retailer's policy first. See returns information.

Caring for Upholstered Furniture

A few habits keep sofas looking new longer. Vacuum crevices weekly with an upholstery attachment, flip and rotate removable cushions every couple of weeks so they wear evenly, and blot spills immediately rather than rubbing. Keep furniture out of strong direct sunlight to limit fading, and in damp spaces keep humidity in check with a dehumidifier to protect fabric and frames. Check the care code on the label: W means water-based cleaners, S means solvent-based cleaners, W/S allows either, and X means vacuum only.

Layout Ideas by Room

Match the piece to the room's job:

  • Living room: face seating toward the main focal point, such as a TV, fireplace or window, and float the sofa off the wall if the room is large.
  • Basement: modular sectionals create a family lounge; pair with a dehumidifier to protect fabrics from damp air.
  • Bedroom or guest room: a sofa bed gives a reading spot by day and a bed at night.

Finish the space with layered lighting so the seating area works for reading, conversation and movie nights.

Quick answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How much space should I leave around a sofa?

Leave about 30–36 inches for main walkways and 14–18 inches between the sofa and a coffee table so people can move and reach drinks comfortably.

Is a modular sectional worth it?

For renters, frequent movers and anyone who likes to rearrange, yes. Modular pieces fit through tighter doorways and can be reconfigured into different shapes as your space changes.

Is corduroy a good sofa fabric?

Corduroy is soft, warm and forgiving of everyday wear. The pile can flatten in high-use seats, so brushing and rotating cushions keeps it looking even.

Can a sofa bed be used every night?

Some can, but most are designed for occasional guests. If it will be slept on nightly, prioritize the mattress or sleeping-surface quality listed by the retailer.

Who handles delivery and returns?

The retailer you buy from. HomeWellnessDeal links to third-party stores, so always confirm delivery times, assembly and return windows on the product page before checkout.

What is the most durable sofa fabric?

Tightly woven synthetic performance fabrics are typically the most resistant to stains and wear, which suits homes with kids and pets.

How long should a sofa last?

With regular care, a well-built sofa commonly lasts many years. Frame construction, cushion density and how heavily it is used make the biggest difference.
